Bio Age Model

Tracks changes in modifiable physiological measures across 12 key biomarkers, body composition, fitness, blood and vital signs.

How it works. Independently verified biomarkers are input and weighted to calculate Bio Age. The divergence chart shows the difference between Bio Age and chronological age at each measured point. Zero means Bio Age equals chronological age; positive values mean biologically older; negative values mean biologically younger. Divergence is capped at ±20 years. Any dotted extension is an extrapolation only.

Illustrative example. The horizontal scale is chronological age and the vertical scale is the difference between Bio Age and chronological age. At age 42, a Bio Age of 52 is +10 years, so the measured red point sits at +10. If that same divergence were simply carried forward linearly, it remains +10: at chronological age 60 the corresponding Bio Age would be 70. The dotted horizontal line illustrates only that constant difference. It is not a forecast that the individual's Bio Age will actually remain 10 years older, nor does it predict future health, lifespan or life expectancy.
