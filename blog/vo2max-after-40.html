TEST, TRACK, IMPROVE

How to measure VO2max

There are three common approaches.

1. Laboratory testing

A direct VO2max test uses a treadmill or cycle ergometer, a face mask and breath-by-breath analysis. It can provide a detailed assessment of oxygen uptake, heart-rate response and exercise performance.

2. Submaximal testing

A submaximal protocol estimates VO2max from exercise performance and heart-rate response without requiring maximal effort. It can be useful for tracking change, although it is less direct than laboratory testing.

3. Wearable estimates

Some watches and fitness devices estimate VO2max from pace, heart rate and training data. These estimates can help identify trends, but they should not be treated as equivalent to a properly controlled laboratory assessment.

Whichever method is used, the key is consistency. Repeating a comparable test under similar conditions makes the trend more useful.

A single score is a snapshot. A sequence of scores shows direction.

FROM DATA TO ACTION

A practical four-step framework

1. Establish the baseline.
Measure VO2max alongside relevant health, lifestyle and body-composition data. Record current training, recovery and constraints.

2. Identify the limiting factor.
The issue may be low aerobic capacity, poor recovery, excess body fat, weak lower-body strength or inconsistent training. Guessing wastes time.

3. Apply the intervention.
Build an appropriate combination of aerobic conditioning, resistance training, nutrition, sleep and stress control.

4. Verify the result.
Repeat the relevant assessments. If performance has not improved, change the plan rather than defending it.

FAQ

What is a good VO2max after 40?

There is no single score that defines “good” for every person. Results should be interpreted against age, sex, training history and test method. The first practical objective is to move out of a low fitness category, then improve or preserve the result through progressive aerobic training and consistent recovery.

Can VO2max increase after 50?

Yes, VO2max can improve after 50 when training is appropriate and repeated consistently. The potential change depends on starting fitness, health, body composition, exercise history and recovery. Progress should be gradual. A controlled baseline test and repeat assessment can show whether the programme is producing measurable adaptation.

Is VO2max the same as biological age?

No. VO2max is one marker of cardiorespiratory fitness, not a complete biological age assessment. A broader biological age test should consider multiple laboratory and functional measures. VO2max can contribute valuable information because it reflects integrated performance under exercise stress, but it should be interpreted alongside other health data.

Is strength training important for healthspan after 50?

Yes. Strength training supports muscle retention, balance, force production and everyday function. It complements aerobic conditioning rather than replacing it. A healthspan-focused programme generally needs both cardiorespiratory fitness and strength, alongside nutrition, sleep, stress control and body-composition management.

How often should VO2max be tested?

The appropriate testing interval depends on the purpose, programme and test method. Testing too frequently may not allow enough time for meaningful adaptation. A professional may use a baseline assessment and repeat it after a structured training period, while wearable estimates can be used more regularly to observe trends.
