Level 1 Bio Age Model

Three domains, seven weighted markers, one composite index. Body composition from a Morpho scan, vital signs from a blood pressure and resting heart rate reading, and a biomechanical domain built on a function test and a postural analysis.

How this tier works. Level 1 measures what can be captured in a single session with no laboratory. A Morpho scan supplies the body composition figures a DEXA scan would otherwise provide. Blood pressure and resting heart rate supply the vital signs. A floor-rise function test and a postural analysis supply the biomechanical numbers. Everything is weighted to 100 points, rolled into a composite, and converted into an Age 1 figure. Without the fitness and blood domains, a Level 1 result is always provisional.

The Old Man Test, explained

The Old Man Test is the sitting-rising test. It takes under a minute, needs no equipment and no laboratory, and it is a functional measure associated with mortality in observational research. The client sits on the floor and stands back up, and the movement is scored out of 10.

How it is scored, step by step

Start each half of the movement at 5 points. Sitting down: lower yourself to the floor without touching anything, and deduct 1 point for every support you use, whether a hand, a forearm, a knee, or the side of a leg. Standing up: return to standing the same way, and deduct 1 point again for each support. Throughout both halves, deduct 0.5 for any moment where balance is lost or the movement becomes unstable. The movement should be continuous, with no pause to gather yourself.

So a client who sits and rises cleanly scores 10. A client who uses one hand on the way down and loses balance once on the way up scores 8.5. A client who needs a hand and a knee in both directions scores 6 before any balance deductions are taken. Hands must not touch the floor or the knee, and the movement must not be split into separate sittings and risings.

Safety. The test is performed on a padded surface with something solid within reach, and anyone with a recent injury, joint replacement or balance disorder should be assessed in clinic rather than at home.

Reference scores by age and sex have been derived from 6,141 adults (Brito et al., 2019). The band cut-offs used for scoring this marker in the model are to be confirmed, and the score above is capped to the model's four bands.

Why these weights

Approved by Lee on 14 Oct 2026: functional ability is emphasised over static posture; scale-derived estimates receive limited weighting. These are design weights informed by evidence, not independently validated coefficients.

Composite Level 1

The composite is the weighted average of the marker scores, out of 100. Fifty is the neutral point: a composite of 50 means Age 1 equals chronological age.

Age 1 = actual age − [ (composite − 50) ÷ 50 × 20 ]

Worked example. A composite of 75 gives 75 − 50 = 25, then 25 ÷ 50 = 0.5, then 0.5 × 20 = 10, so the age adjustment is 10 years younger. The same maths runs in reverse below 50. The adjustment is capped at plus or minus 20 years.

Age 1 divergence

Age 1 is plotted against chronological age at every assessment. Zero means Age 1 equals chronological age; positive means biologically older; negative means biologically younger.

Illustrative example. The dotted horizontal line carries the current difference forward and is not a forecast. It does not predict future health, lifespan or life expectancy.
