Optimal Reel Length (2026 data)

There's no single "default" length, the right duration depends on the job the post is doing:

  • Discovery content (reaching non-followers), 15, 30 seconds. This is Instagram's primary discovery band; shorter Reels get pushed to Explore/the Reels tab far more than longer ones.
  • Educational/explainer content, 30, 45 seconds. Enough room to say something real without losing the discovery benefit.
  • 10 Tenets series, fixed at 30, 35 seconds (already set, see Tenets tab).
  • Hook in the first 3 seconds matters more than exact length, roughly half of viewers decide to stay or leave in that window, regardless of format.
  • Going much longer than ~90 seconds opts a post out of discovery, it mostly stays inside your existing follower graph instead of reaching new people.

Paid Ads, Meta (Facebook/Instagram)

Critical: paid ads are reviewed under much stricter rules than organic posts. Meta's 2026 health/wellness policy bans "guaranteed" results claims, time-bound transformation claims, and before/after imagery, enforced by semantic AI that catches meaning, not just literal wording. Your organic banner ("REVERSE YOUR BIOLOGICAL AGE BY 10 YEARS. GUARANTEED!") cannot run as paid ad copy as written. Proof/Authority Filming Guides

You don't need to be an expert on these apps, you need to know exactly what to screen-record and how. Data at-source is the strongest visual you have, it can't be faked and it doesn't need explaining.

Whoop

Screen-record, not a photo
  1. Open the Whoop app, go to your daily Recovery or Strain screen.
  2. Start your phone's screen recorder before you tap into the app, so the reveal is captured live.
  3. Scroll slowly through Recovery percentage, HRV trend, and Sleep Performance, pause 1 to 2 seconds on each number.
  4. Film this as B-roll under your voiceover, or hold the phone up to camera and narrate live as you scroll, both work.

Huawei Health App

Pair the data with the activity it's tracking
  1. Open Huawei Health, go to the workout or daily activity summary screen right after finishing a session.
  2. Screen-record the summary: steps, heart rate zones, calories, sleep score, whichever is most relevant that day.
  3. Cut this screen-recording against real footage of the activity itself, the data means more paired with the effort that produced it.

DEXA Scan Lab Data

The report itself is the visual
  1. Photograph or screen-record the actual PDF or dashboard from BodyView, scrolling slowly through body fat %, lean mass, bone density.
  2. Crop or blur any personal ID details (name, date of birth) before this goes anywhere near a post.
  3. Pair with a same-day gym or training clip so the number has a face and a body attached to it, not just a spreadsheet.

Kids Interview, Full Question Script

📌 Goal: one genuinely compelling 45-minute session with all 3 kids together, Sophie (@sophiestonehouse), Aiden (@aiden_stonehouse), and Gracie (@graciestonehouse), producing enough warm, funny, and legacy-driven footage to bank multiple future Thread C cutdowns. 📌 The real timeline, so questions land accurately: before 2015, you were physically active with them, farm work at weekends, dirt bikes and buggies, but still out of shape and eating and drinking sub-optimally despite training. Goal here is just to get everyone relaxed and talking naturally before anything reflective. Don't rush past this section.

  2. "What's the most embarrassing thing I've ever done in public in the name of health?"

  4. "What's one food you've never seen me eat, and never will?"

Section 2, Before, The Farm Years (roughly 8 minutes)

This covers the era before 2015, active with you physically, but not yet the transformation. Let them describe it honestly, this isn't a section to rush past or soften.

  1. "What do you remember about the farm at weekends, the dirt bikes, the buggies, before any of this health stuff started?"
  2. "Back then, would you have called me fit, or just active? What's the difference in your memory?"
  3. "Did you ever notice, even as kids, that I was working out but not really looking after myself otherwise?"
  4. "What's your honest memory of how I ate and drank back then, compared to now?"

Section 3, The Turning Point, 2015 (roughly 8 minutes)

This is the most sensitive section. Let it be honest, not dramatic. You were 15, 13, and 12. Give them room to answer at whatever depth feels right, don't push if it feels raw.

  1. "What do you remember about that period, when everything changed for our family?"
  2. "Did you notice a difference in me once your mum and I split, in how I started living?"
  3. "Was there a specific moment you remember thinking, 'Dad's actually changing'?"
  4. "Looking back now, what do you think that year did for me?"

Section 4, The Rebirth, 2015 to Now (roughly 8 minutes)

This is the transformation arc itself, watched year on year, including the book.

  1. "What's it been like watching me go from where I was to where I am now, over all these years?"
  2. "Do you remember when I started writing the book? What did you think at the time?"
  3. "Is there a specific year or moment where you thought, 'this isn't a phase, this is who Dad actually is now'?"
  4. "What do your friends say when they find out what I do now, compared to what they'd have said about me before 2015?"

Section 5, The Roast Round (roughly 8 minutes)

This is the section that gets shared. Let them go, don't defend yourself too hard, the goal is genuine laughs.

  1. "If you had to describe my supplement cabinet to a stranger, what would you say?"
  2. "What's the most 'Dad' thing I've said about ageing?"

  4. "On a scale of 1 to 10, how likely am I to bring up my DEXA scan at a family dinner?"

Closing section, both the legacy and the fact this is still ongoing for both of you. Let this land with real emotion if it comes, don't rush to wrap up.

  1. "What's something about how you live now that you can directly trace back to watching the last 10 years, not just who I always was?"
